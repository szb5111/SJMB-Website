The Problem

Chaos/Control

Anyone can stack riffs and call it “a song,” but if it doesn’t take the listener on an adventure, it falls flat.

Big, wild ideas are hard to turn into something thoughtful and captivating without it sounding like a jumbled mess.

Verdict Riff Salad

That’s the difference between:

The goal

An intentional Kubrick masterpiece

Every frame earns its place. Nothing is there by accident, and you feel the shape of it before you can explain it.

or
The trap

A gaudy Michael Bay car crash

Cool for about ninety seconds, then it’s just loud: A five-minute explosion with nothing holding it together.

That’s where I nerd out. I think about music the way a director thinks about a film: the tension, the release, the scene changes that keep your fans glued to their headphones.

If you’ve got a ton of influences, my goal is to make them feel like they grew up together. If your “big cinematic idea” is more spectacle than story, I’ll shape it into something people come back to again and again and again.

Because for me, all the nerdy little details serve one purpose:

Holding a mirror to intention…

and letting it crawl through.

The Approach

Your Goal,
My Purpose

Working with me isn’t a conveyor belt. Whether you’re in the room or sending tracks from across the ocean:

I want you to feel like I’m part of your band.

That means asking questions, digging into your influences, tossing around ideas, and caring about the details as much as you do.

Oh, and probably making fun of each other. A lot.

  1. 01

    Storyboard first

    We map what the listener should feel in every bar before a single part gets tracked.

  2. 02

    Serve the song

    Whatever is done on the song serves the song; not our ego.

  3. 03

    Extra member

    I co-write, arrange and play. Screams, mathy leads, synth ambience: whatever the story needs.

  4. 04

    Open book

    No gatekeeping, no velvet rope. You get the real answer behind the process, every time.

Long story short: the process should feel energising and personal, not rushed or transactional.

The Sauce

What I Do

Four ways in. Most projects start with a call and end up somewhere none of us predicted, in the best way.

01

Production

Sketch → Masterpiece

The Holy Grail. Choose-your-adventure, all-access, I-want-it-that-way. From pre-production through mastering, we take your song from a voice memo to the thing that defines “this era of you”!

  • Pre-production & storyboarding
  • Arrangement & co-writing
  • Tracking & performance coaching
  • Full delivery
02

Mixing

Show you who you are

Just like what defines a great director: “I take what you give me, and show you who you are”! With a couple of solid references, we get your tracks living in the right colorspace.

  • Emotional automation
  • Depth & dimension
  • Reference matching
  • Stems & recalls
03

Mastering

Good → Undeniable

Good is good, but it could be better. The mix is great, it’s just missing some luster. Send it over and let’s get it sitting on par with the heavy hitters in your scene.

  • Loudness & translation
  • Tonal polish
  • Sequencing & spacing
  • All delivery formats
04

About

Producer.
Mix Engineer.
Riff Wrangler.

Shane J. M. Brower is more than just an engineer. He’s an Audio Aficionado out of Jersey City, NJ, partnering with indie and metal artists to enhance their current sound and evolve it. He’s your band’s extra member, consolidating and actualising your ideas into emotional rollercoasters.

After decades writing songs and self-recording, Shane became drawn to the importance of storytelling: using production to further the impact of the message being told. For years he mixed live band recordings, making tracks with loads of bleed sound like full-blown albums. That devotion to polishing raw, live performance honed his ability to sculpt an artist’s sonic vision.

Your music is the deepest reflection of yourself. By leaning into your influences (bands, artists, video games, films) you can make music that truly represents your vision. Through storytelling and harnessing that influence, he’ll have you wielding your voice as a weapon.
